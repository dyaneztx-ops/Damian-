After flipping properties, I've learned that the right small fixes often beat a big renovation. Here's how I'd think about it.

## Usually worth it

- **Fresh, neutral paint** in high-traffic rooms. It's one of the cheapest ways to make a home photograph and show better.
- **Lighting**: replace burned-out bulbs and dated fixtures in the kitchen and entry.
- **Small repairs buyers notice**: dripping faucets, running toilets, broken blinds, cracked switch plates, sticky doors.
- **HVAC service**: in Houston, buyers *always* ask about the AC. A recent service receipt builds confidence.
- **Curb appeal**: trimmed shrubs, fresh mulch, a clean front door, and a power-washed walkway.
- **Deep clean**, including windows, grout, and baseboards.

## Often NOT worth it before listing

- **Full kitchen or bath remodels.** You rarely get back what you spend, and buyers may want their own style.
- **New flooring throughout**, unless it's damaged or very dated. A credit can sometimes make more sense.
- **Upgrades that don't fit the neighborhood.** Over-improving for the area rarely pays.

## Consider a pre-listing inspection

On older homes, a pre-listing inspection lets you find and fix (or price in) problems *before* a buyer's inspector finds them during the option period, when you have less leverage.

## Disclose, disclose, disclose

Texas sellers must complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice. Be upfront about known issues, past repairs, and any flooding. It protects you and builds trust with buyers.
